At a glance

## Swim in four lines

Who

A lover who goes to the depths to feel all of it, the romance and the tragedy, and comes back with songs.

Sound

Soulful indie R&B. A raspy, intimate voice sunk into washed-out reverb, washed guitar, human drums, real keys, textural synths.

Movie

A psychedelic Richard Linklater film. Coming of age, consciousness, and romance, shot like a festival darling.

Enemy

Content culture. Music made to grab attention instead of hold it.

### The one-liner

“A lover learning to feel everything, in a world built to scroll past it.”

“Learning to feel everything” is the inner journey. “A world built to scroll past it” is the outer enemy. Every release and every post should trace back to this line.

The casual shorthand for the sound is bedroom soul juice.

The movie

## The soundtrack to a psychedelic Linklater film

Part Waking Life, part Boyhood, part Before Sunrise, with the look of an indie festival darling. It’s about the human experience: romance, coming of age, and the big questions of life and consciousness, told poetically.

### Heightened, not invented

The world is real in its emotions. Light, sound, and cinematography push it toward the surreal so those emotions can land. Nothing fantastical, nothing in costume.

### Literary touchstones

- The poetry and romance of Shantaram

- The wisdom of Pema Chödrön, and her steadiness in the face of suffering

- The aching beauty of Kaveh Akbar’s Martyr!

The character

## The Lover, with the Sage underneath

Lover
dominant

Romanticizes life. Wants to feel it all, fully, and to live a rich life.

Sage
secondary

Seeks truth through consciousness and art. Wants self-understanding and self-actualization. There’s a mystic streak, but it stays grounded and never becomes a spiritual costume.

Creator
the craft

Building worlds around a concept is how Swim expresses himself. It’s his method rather than his motivation.

Plot

Voyage and Return. He goes into the depths (the night, the road, the hard feelings) and comes home changed, with a song.

Inner villain

His own conditioning.

Outer enemy

Content culture.

From the notebook

“I’m the lover, the mystic, the creator. I find romance in life, in the tragedy and in emotional expression. I go to the depths to find it, express it through concept, and bring those concepts together through art. I see art as the medium to work through suffering, the tragedy of life.”

Emotional core

## It aches, it moves, and it takes you somewhere.

Aches

The melancholy. Beauty found inside the tragedy, “beautiful from a different place.” Tender, honest, raw.

Moves

The vitality. Groove, soul, a little swagger. The saddest song still has a pulse, and the dance track still starts from the melancholy.

Takes you
somewhere

Something soulful that people can attach to and lose themselves in: rhythms to sink into, songs that travel.

The stance is grounded. Swim feels everything without performing it.

### The test

Ask it of every song, every visual, every post. Does it ache? Does it move? Does it take me somewhere? A track that only aches drifts into the background. A track that only moves is pop, which is the lane Swim is running away from.

From the notebook

“Even when it’s danceable, even when it’s money, I come from a melancholic place, because it’s the feeling that this is beautiful from a different place. It’s soulful. It’s slightly swaggy.”

The sound

## The core: soulful indie R&B

Somewhere between Mac Miller, River Tiber, Dijon, Mk.gee, and Leif Vollebekk. Atmospheric, textural, and rich in production, and never pop. The whole world exists as a playground for the voice to live inside of.

### Apollo in the approach, Dionysus in the sound

The approach is Apollonian. Swim treats the craft, the art, and the poetry as high art, made to outlast shifts in taste while moving with the cultural forefront. The voice comes from the same place: melancholic, poetic reflection, rather than the direct, boyish storytelling of Steve Lacy or Jon Bellion.

The sound is Dionysian. The arrangement and the mix are vital, raw, and alive, the way Dijon and Jai Paul arrange and mix: metallic guitar, drums that go off, unexpected turns, nothing polished into place.

Considered in what it says, alive in how it sounds. Check every track against both halves: is the writing reflective and poetic enough, and is the production alive enough?

### Signature ingredients

If someone copied these, people should notice.

- The voice as an instrument. Intimate, raspy, laidback, mixed into the track under washed-out plate reverb.

- Washed, metallic guitar.

- Human drums with real feel.

- Texture. Textural synths, real keys, and small chaotic sound details.

- The pen. Reflective, poetic writing about consciousness, longing, and romance, with Mac’s flow for swagger.

### Production palette

Voice

Laidback and raspy, which is what lets it live inside the mix rather than sit on top of it. Intimate and close, in washed-out plate reverb. It comes from melancholic, poetic reflection. The high, emotive moments are where it breaks loose.

Guitar

Washed and metallic, groove first. The Dijon and Mk.gee edge, with a hint of surf in the wash.

Drums

The style changes from song to song: a simple indie kit, a chopped break, or crushed Mk.gee and Dijon-style drums (Justin Bieber’s “Yukon” lives here). They are always human, textured, and warm, never the clean pop drum, and free to go off when a song needs release.

Keys & synths

Real keys. Beautiful, textural synths. 80s warmth.

Texture

Atmospheric and layered, with small chaotic sound elements. Imperfections stay in when they make it feel alive.

Writing

Consciousness, longing, romance, the unfolding. Melancholic, poetic reflection, never literal and never direct storytelling. Always from his own experience and realizations, never instruction. Mac’s flows carry the swagger.

Never

Pop sheen. A vocal parked on top of the mix. Hooks engineered for reels. Literal lyrics. Direct, boyish storytelling.

Not this lane

Dijon and Mk.gee rawness × River Tiber texture × Mac Miller’s pen, with Leif Vollebekk’s honesty underneath.

Side projects

## Two more rooms in the same world

Jazz and dance are separate projects, released as their own bodies of work. They share Swim’s world and emotional core, and they sit alongside the main sound rather than inside it.

### The jazz project

Live, rhythm-led music to lose yourself in. The drums sit at the center, played live, layered and shifting, and the songs travel through sections instead of looping.

References

Hiatus Kaiyote, Yussef Dayes, Moses Yoofee Trio.

The rule

The rhythm can be complex. The feeling has to stay simple enough to hold. Hiatus Kaiyote do this well: the groove wanders everywhere, and the voice and melody are always something to hold onto.

### The dance project: motion as release

The same feeling as the core songs, moved through the body instead of held still. These tracks are about emotional release through movement itself. They are not party music.

The core already sounds alive. In the dance project, that vitality moves into the body: synths up front, the tempo up, movement in charge. The voice still comes from the same reflective place, and the melancholy stays. It’s grief, longing, and love you finally get to move through rather than escape from, the Lover’s want to feel everything carried into the body.

Sound

Synth-led and upbeat, with an 80s and synthwave pulse and driving drums. The voice is free to soar here, where the core keeps it tucked into the mix.

Collaborators

Sexpop (Austin). Urban Heat (synthwave).

The test

It still has to ache. If a track only makes people want to party, it belongs to someone else. If it makes them move because of what they feel, it’s Swim.

Never

Partying for its own sake, hype drops, empty euphoria, hands-in-the-air energy.

The look

Motion rather than the party. A body moving alone in the dark, the night drive, light streaking past, the sweat after the release. Never the crowd shot.

The audience

## Why people follow

Sameness

“He feels what I feel.” Swim says out loud what they feel and rarely say: the longing, the melancholy, the search. He does it vulnerably and humbly, never preachy or polished, so they recognize themselves in him.

Attraction

“It makes my life feel like a film.” Nostalgia for now. The sound and the visuals turn a drive, a walk, or a late night into a scene they already miss while it’s still happening.

Aspiration

“I want to be that free.” Swim feels everything and says it out loud. Fans want that freedom for themselves, and the creatives among them see a model for making art instead of content.

Guidance

“He’s on the same path, and he tells me what he finds.” Swim writes about art and consciousness from his own experience: what he noticed, what cracked open, what he’s still unsure about. He shares realizations and never teaches. There’s no “you should” anywhere in it.

Every post should give people at least one of these. A post that gives none is just content.

### Nostalgia for now

“This life is a movie, man.”

Swim gives people nostalgia for the moment they’re in: the sense, while it’s happening, that this is already a scene worth remembering. Behind it is a bigger belief. Reality is malleable, and a life lived with attention is a work of art. The Lover romanticizes his own life, and the listener walks away ready to romanticize theirs.

### What a fan feels is true about themselves

“I feel everything. I have taste. I’m doing the inner work for real, and I want to be free enough to say all of it.”

The long version: someone who feels deeply, has refined taste in art, holds the full range of emotional experience, pays grounded attention to their body and inner life (never the spiritual costume), feels longing, and wants to express it raw and free.

### The promise

Every release is worth your attention. Each piece is made as art and held to a standard. The visuals build a world that serves the music, never the algorithm.

Beliefs

## What Swim stands for

We believe

- in feeling everything, fully

- in exploring the nature of consciousness

- that art can transform suffering and make sense of the senseless

- that the world is beautiful and full of suffering, and that this is life

- that reality is malleable, and a life lived with attention is a work of art

We don’t believe

- in making things to grab attention instead of holding it

- in the spiritual costume, awakening worn as an aesthetic

- that faster and more is better

- in numbing out or pretending everything is fine

### The enemy

Literal, attention-grabbing pop music and reels. The content engine of fast-moving media.

The look

## Raw, surreal, artful, cinematic, honest

Refined taste. Apollonian, with room to flex. Every visual is a scene from the same movie: cohesive short-film stories made with close attention to cinematography.

### Shooting rules

Honest

Real skin, real places, no heavy retouching.

Cinematic

Light that comes from a real source in the scene. Shoot in the Linklater hours: dusk, the long night, first light.

Surreal

One heightened element per frame, whether light, color, or time. Never fantasy props.

Restraint,
then release

Still, composed frames, with one moment of motion or blur in each piece.

One world

Short films rather than posts. Every piece belongs to the same world.

### Water

The name already does brand work. The depths, the wet reverb, the washed guitar, the flow, the voyage. Water and immersion can run through the visuals as a recurring motif.

Showing up

## Consistent without becoming the enemy

Swim’s enemy is content culture, yet audiences still grow when an artist shows up often in one recognizable format. The way through is to make the repeatable thing art-shaped. Every post is a fragment of the movie and never bait.

### Possible formats

- A recurring short-film vignette

- Bedroom sessions, shot in the world’s look

- A poem or voice-memo drop

Pick one and commit to it.

The game

## Best art now, then the audience, then the revenue

The strategy is to make the best art possible right now, build an audience from that editorial work, and let revenue follow the audience.
