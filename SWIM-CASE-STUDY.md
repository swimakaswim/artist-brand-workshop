# Swim — worked example of turning discovery into a guide

## What this example is and isn't

The complete finished Swim guide is included in examples/swim-brand-guide.html. Its original section structure is: At a glance, The movie, The character, Emotional core, The sound, Side projects, The audience, Beliefs, The look, Showing up, The game.

The notes below reconstruct the connections visible in that finished document. They do not claim to recover the original interview, a hidden decision log, or the order in which the artist reached each conclusion. Swim's answers are examples, not defaults for another artist. The new interview workflow in WORKSHOP.md is an adaptation informed by the NAS study and this finished artifact.

## 1. A central sentence ties several dimensions together

Swim's central line is:

> A lover learning to feel everything, in a world built to scroll past it.

The line combines a character tendency, an inner journey, and a cultural tension. It gives the guide a usable center rather than only a description of musical genre. The inner journey is learning to feel; the outer opposition is an attention economy that encourages people to move past feeling.

**What transfers:** find a compact sentence supported by several parts of the artist's actual work and life. Test whether it changes a decision.

**What doesn't transfer:** lover, emotional depth, and opposition to content culture. A different artist might be playful, confrontational, precise, communal, fictional, or spectacle-driven.

## 2. The movie becomes a concrete creative reference

The guide describes a psychedelic Richard Linklater film: romance, coming of age, and consciousness, with the treatment of an independent festival film. It names different contributions from Waking Life, Boyhood, and Before Sunrise, alongside literary references.

The important move is not name-dropping a director. It is specifying the kind of human experience and cinematic treatment the references contribute. The world is emotionally real and can become surreal through light, sound, and cinematography. That supplies a boundary: heightened feeling without fantasy costume.

**What transfers:** ask what each reference contributes, then turn that into a world rule.

**What doesn't transfer:** the director, books, grounded reality, or prohibition on fantasy. A conceptual artist may need the opposite boundary.

## 3. Character describes motivation rather than job title

Swim's guide places Lover first and Sage underneath. Creator describes the method of expression: building worlds around concepts. It distinguishes the drive to feel and understand from the fact that the artist creates things.

The guide uses Voyage and Return as a narrative shape: going into the night, the road, or hard feelings and returning with songs. It names personal conditioning as an internal obstacle and content culture as an external one.

**What transfers:** separate motivation, expression, and obstacle; ask whether a narrative lens clarifies real material.

**What doesn't transfer:** that archetype pairing, narrative shape, or any requirement to have an enemy. These are editorial lenses, not diagnoses.

## 4. Sound makes the world audible

The at-a-glance description is soulful indie R&B: a raspy, intimate voice, washed-out reverb, washed guitar, human drums, real keys, and textural synths. These are concrete ingredients a collaborator can hear or act on. The casual shorthand, "bedroom soul juice," gives people a less formal way to describe it.

The relationship matters: intimate voice and immersed textures support the guide's emotional world. The full sound section supplies more detail than the headline alone.

**What transfers:** write both a public description and a working recipe. Test what remains recognizable across different songs.

**What doesn't transfer:** any particular genre, vocal quality, instrument, or production texture.

## 5. Audience is an emotional relationship

The guide describes what listeners recognize in themselves and what the artist offers them. One of its strongest ideas is "nostalgia for now": helping a listener experience the present as a scene worth remembering. Guidance comes from sharing discoveries from the same path, rather than adopting a teacher's authority.

This translates an audience feeling into a voice boundary: sharing what the artist noticed and is still unsure about, without prescribing how everyone else should live.

**What transfers:** identify a listener situation, a reason to return, and the relationship the artist is willing to sustain.

**What doesn't transfer:** a specific listener identity or a claim that another artist's fans want introspection. New projects should label untested audience hypotheses.

## 6. Beliefs explain choices and exclusions

Swim's guide connects feeling fully, exploring consciousness, and making sense of suffering with resistance to attention bait, numbing out, and spiritual appearance without substance.

These are useful when they constrain work: an attractive concept that treats spirituality as a costume conflicts with the guide; a fashionable post that grabs attention without holding it may conflict too. The beliefs give the visual and editorial choices a reason.

**What transfers:** a belief should change a choice; check whether its opposite would change the project's meaning.

**What doesn't transfer:** the specific belief system. The artist's own examples must establish theirs.

## 7. Feelings become literal production rules

The look is described as raw, surreal, artful, cinematic, and honest. The guide then translates those words into choices:

| Principle | Execution visible in the guide |
|---|---|
| Honest | Real skin and real places; avoid heavy retouching |
| Cinematic | A real source of light in the scene; dusk, night, or first light |
| Surreal | One heightened element in light, color, or time |
| Restraint and release | Composed stillness with a moment of motion or blur |
| One world | Pieces that feel like related short films |

Water can recur because it connects the name with depth, immersion, washed textures, and the voyage idea. A motif is stronger when it has several reasons to belong.

**What transfers:** feeling → reason → visible rule → avoidances. Brief an actual shooter or designer.

**What doesn't transfer:** water, blur, naturalistic light, or these adjectives as universal signs of artistic quality.

## 8. The guide keeps a productive tension visible

Swim's guide opposes content culture while acknowledging that audiences grow through recognizable ways of showing up. Its answer is to make the recurring thing feel like part of the art: short-film vignettes, bedroom sessions, or poem/voice-memo drops.

The original guide lists possible formats and says to pick one. That is an open execution decision, not a completed content system. The new workshop closes this gap by selecting one feasible format with the artist and drafting its first three installments and production plan.

**What transfers:** resolve a tension with a working design choice instead of hiding it with nicer prose.

**What doesn't transfer:** a prescribed format, daily posting, or a promise that any particular schedule will grow an audience.

## 9. A finished guide is not automatically a complete asset kit

Swim's document is a coherent strategic and editorial guide. A friend may additionally need a shoot brief, voice examples, explicit visual directions, a working sound recipe, and first actions. The workshop expands the structure into those deliverables.

An AI can propose palettes, typography directions, and mood-board briefs. Those remain proposals until reviewed against references and actual production. A finished logo, licensed photo library, recorded sound, or hosted password gate is not created just by describing it.

## The transferable build sequence

1. Collect specific answers, work examples, tastes, constraints, and boundaries.
2. Look for a central idea that explains several of them at once.
3. Separate motivation, world, emotional core, signature ingredients, and listener relationship.
4. Name contradictions that affect the work and resolve them with the artist.
5. Translate each abstract principle into a creative rule and a concrete example.
6. Assemble a guide that a new collaborator can understand without seeing the interview.
7. Extend it into practical briefs, examples, and a feasible first production plan.
8. Test consistency, specificity, truthfulness, and feasibility; preserve unknowns honestly.

That sequence is the adaptation. Another artist should end up with a different world, sound, voice, and set of rules.
