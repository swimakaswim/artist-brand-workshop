# Fetish: Mindset Is Everything, An Approach To Music and Life
**Length / Source:** Episode 15 | Tue, 26 Nov 2024 | 66m | source: whisper
**Thesis (1 sentence):** Fetish argues that a sustainable creative career is driven primarily by internal psychology, not external tactics — specifically, redefining success as the ongoing process of creating rather than any milestone (since the goalpost always moves), and consciously "reprogramming" your own self-narrative, a skill he says was forged by surviving genuine childhood instability.

## Key claims & mental models
- A difficult, unstable childhood (divorced parents, a family cancer diagnosis, a mother with schizophrenia, roughly 14 house moves by age 14) is presented as the origin of an early, persistent pull toward existential/big-picture questions — a pattern he says is common among artists from unstable backgrounds, when they have enough luck and support to "come out of it" intact rather than be broken by it.
- Frames his own personality as roughly 20% innate temperament and 80% shaped by upbringing (an explicit 80/20 guess, not a hard claim) — used to argue that the "superpowers" (adaptability, reframing ability, comfort with non-attachment) he draws on professionally were built, not born.
- The central mental model is the "boulder" metaphor: satisfaction has to come from the process of pushing toward a goal, not the moment of reaching it, because the goalpost silently moves again the instant you cross it — a dopamine/anticipation mechanism he treats as a hardwired fact about motivation, not a platitude.
- A mentor's intervention (David Alexander of Icon Collective) reframed a period of feeling perpetually behind by walking through the concrete facts of his actual week (signed to a real label, playing real festivals) and pointing out he'd already crossed the finish line he'd originally set for himself, months or years earlier, without noticing.
- Deliberately seeks out "the fringes" across music, film, and books rather than consensus favorites — his reasoning is that heavily-consumed mainstream work has already been fully picked over by everyone else, while boundary-pushing/underexposed work still rewards genuine curiosity and personal discovery.
- Explicitly rejects a single flattened personal brand in favor of showing multiple authentic facets (the surfer/beach friend, the existential-conversation friend, the high-energy performer) — acknowledges this reads as harder to market or "disjointed" compared to one clean hook, but frames it as the more honest and, increasingly, the more contemporary form of authenticity.
- Redefines success explicitly away from money or career milestones: describes himself as "the most successful rich person" he knows because he gets to make music, play shows, and spend time with people he loves on his own terms — career "lines in the sand" are still worth pursuing, but aren't where fulfillment actually lives.
- Values catalog depth over any single viral moment — cites artists with long, evolving discographies (Chris Lake, Taiki New Light) as more rewarding to fans who dig past the 1-2 hits, and states an explicit personal goal of building toward 1,000 released songs.
- Names content-creation/self-marketing as a genuine weak point he's actively working to improve rather than opting out of, arguing that fully opting out of modern artist-as-content-creator demands is realistically only viable for a vanishingly small tier of talent.

## Concrete tactics (do-this)
- Run a "top 20" exercise: quickly list your 20 favorite songs, albums, and movies without overthinking the ranking — the resulting pattern (mainstream vs. fringe) is a fast, honest readout of your actual taste, useful for calibrating what you should be making.
- Do a periodic (roughly quarterly, informally scheduled) solo audit: block 4-5 hours alone, journal or free-write on where you are, whether you're aligned with your original path, and where you could improve — treat it as a corrective zoom-out against day-to-day tunnel vision, not a performance review.
- Run the "boulder check" when you feel perpetually behind: write down the concrete, factual specifics of your current week/month (what you shipped, where you played, who you're working with) and compare them against the goal you set when you started — you may find you already crossed the original line and just didn't notice because the goalpost moved.
- Practice "reprogramming": identify one negative self-story you repeat (his example: "I don't have friends because we keep moving") and consciously rewrite it into a functional reframe ("I'm good at adapting to new environments") — repeat the new version until it becomes the default read.
- Track catalog depth as its own long-term metric (total songs released) alongside any individual song's performance, as a hedge against over-indexing on viral hits.

## Highest-leverage insight
> Fetish's "boulder check": when you're stuck in a feeling of perpetual not-enough, write down the plain, factual specifics of what your life actually looks like right now — what you released, where you played last, who you're working with — and compare that list directly against the goal you'd have written down when you started out. His mentor did this to him mid-conversation and the answer was that he'd crossed his own original finish line long before, without ever stopping to register it, because a new, harder goal had silently replaced it the moment he reached the old one. The actionable move isn't "be grateful" in the abstract — it's the literal side-by-side list, done this week, against your own past goals rather than someone else's current highlight reel.

## Notable quotes
- "It's not just the gratitude that you've come and achieved the line. It's that the process of pushing the boulder is the fun part." — Fetish
- "You're halfway up the fucking mountain." — Fetish, quoting his mentor David Alexander
- "The real success was you being a functional, healthy, energetic, and happy person to be pushing the boulder along the way up. That was the success, not the line." — Fetish
