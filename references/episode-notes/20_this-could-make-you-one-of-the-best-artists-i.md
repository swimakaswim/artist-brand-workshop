# What the F*ck to Actually Post for Content as a Music Artist Right Now
**Length / Source:** Tue, 07 Jan 2025 | 24m | source: youtube
**Thesis (1 sentence):** Culture is swinging (as it always does generationally) away from polished/manufactured content toward "anti-marketing" — raw, human, imperfect content — because AI has made perfection cheap and audiences have become marketing-literate enough to detect and discount manufactured content, so artists should build 2-3 repeatable "real" content formats rather than chase polish.

## Key claims & mental models
- Culture cycles in a recurring pendulum between "manufactured/polished/escapist" eras and "real/human/raw" eras roughly every decade, driven by each new generation wanting to visibly differentiate from the one before (traced through the 1950s ad-perfect era → 60s counterculture; disco/blockbuster 70s → punk; big-hair/neon 80s → grunge/reality-TV 90s; late-90s boy-band pop → mid-2000s blog/indie-sleaze era); the Beatles' shift from matching-suit boy-band image to raw, long-haired, live-recorded "Let It Be" is used as a single-band illustration of the same swing.
- This current swing is not primarily caused by AI — AI is an accelerant on top of the same recurring generational pattern, not the root cause.
- He attributes the current intensity (beyond simple generational cycling) to AI making "perfect" content cheap and ubiquitous, which erodes the value of perfection itself, plus a two-sided "everyone learned the playbook" effect: creators all absorbed the same content-optimization tactics (hooks, captions, pattern interrupts, algorithm study), so the playing field stayed level, AND audiences absorbed the same playbook and can now consciously or subconsciously detect when they're being sold to — making genuine truth/trust exponentially more valuable by contrast.
- Reframe of the moment: it isn't really "anti-marketing," it's "anti being marketed to, and pro human connection."
- A brand's "realness" has to be coherent across every surface, not just posts — wardrobe, environment, and styling must support the story (Post Malone's shift from platinum-grill/Louis Vuitton to pickup-truck/Budweiser "everyday Americana" styling is given as a deliberate, coherent rebrand toward a more human story).
- Where an artist is in their career changes which format lands: showing "ordinary life" only reads as interesting once you're already established/extraordinary; for a brand-new artist, ordinary life just blends into everyone else's ordinary life.
- Best new content ideas aren't wholly new — they're old, pre-digital raw formats (Xerox flyers, zines, disposable cameras, VHS, mixtapes, demo tapes) recombined with current platforms/formats; "there's no new ideas, just new combinations of ideas that already exist."

## Concrete tactics (do-this)
- Nine specific "real" content categories to pull from (each paired with an "old version" reference to combine with a modern format): (1) anti-design handcrafted/collage/hand-drawn graphics; (2) real photos/video with harsh flash, red-eye, motion blur, crooked framing; (3) digital artifacts — screenshots of texts, DMs, notes app, voice memos, group chats; (4) work-in-progress — unfinished demos, rejected logos, botched rehearsals, whiteboard notes; (5) the unedited "yap" video — single unbroken phone-camera talking head, no captions/hooks, direct-to-camera like a FaceTime call (flagged as "crushing right now"); (6) genuinely unpolished behind-the-scenes (bad angle, bad audio, visible frustration) instead of staged "pretend the camera isn't there" BTS; (7) styling/environment that's actually lived-in rather than a constructed set; (8) reality-TV style ordinary-life content (works best once you're already established); (9) community-made/UGC content — fan videos, bootlegs, crowd footage, reaction videos folded into your official brand feed.
- Process: pick 3 of the 9 formats (mix and match), commit to them as repeatable formats, map them onto a 30-day content calendar, then after 30 days review both the performance data and how each felt to make — keep the winners, cut or retest the rest.
- Free resources referenced (viewer had to comment on the original video to receive): a 30-day content calendar template, and a document linking to 30 real-world examples of this "real" content style.

## Highest-leverage insight
> Don't try to invent something new every single day. Pick exactly three of the nine listed "real" formats (e.g., the unedited yap video + digital-artifact screenshots + work-in-progress clips), commit to them as your repeatable rotation, and map them onto a 30-day calendar — then judge results by both the data and how each felt to make before deciding what to keep. The specific non-obvious lever is combining an "old" pre-internet raw-media reference (VHS, Xerox, contact sheets, disposable-camera flash) with a current platform format — that pairing reads as genuinely authentic rather than trend-chasing, because it isn't simply copying whatever's currently viral.
