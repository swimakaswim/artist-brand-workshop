# Ep18 — The Difference Between A $50,000 DJ VS $2,000

**Thesis (1 sentence):** The gap between a $2,000-a-show DJ and a $50,000-a-show DJ comes down to having clear, confident answers to a defined set of brand-identity questions — starting with what you genuinely love and how it aligns with your music — not raw musical talent. ## Key claims & mental models
- Central reframe: the $2k-vs-$50k DJ gap isn't about musical talent but about whether the artist (or their team) can answer a defined set of brand-identity questions with clarity.
- Fee is treated as a direct signal of brand strength, not just popularity — grounded in his own years as a talent buyer, where the same room and hour could be offered anywhere from $1,500 to $100,000 depending on projected draw tied to the artist's story and following, not just their production quality.
- Building "a solid business" around music requires treating story and brand as their own discipline with the same seriousness as making the music — packaging and business-building are named as separately "super hard" skills, distinct from making great music itself.
- Top-tier artist behavior is defined by outcomes fans/promoters can see: fans patiently wait for and sell out tickets in minutes, the artist plays the peak/headlining slot instead of the early slot, and the artist sustains financial freedom purely from creative work.
- Brand-question 1 ("what do you love") is explicitly rooted in long-held, pre-existing taste — "ideally something you've loved for a really long time, not just something that's popular today" — echoing the anti-trend-chasing stance found across his other episodes.
- Brand consistency (question 3, per the surviving fragment) is reframed away from repetition: a favorite brand isn't consistent because it repeats the same output, but because everything it offers, however varied, clearly lives inside one coherent brand ecosystem.

## Concrete tactics (do-this)
- Build a personal "mood board" of things you have long-standing, genuine love for (music, brands, movies, product design, other artists) as the literal first step in defining your brand — favor longevity of the love over current popularity.
- Explicitly cross-reference that mood board against your actual music to find where they overlap — the overlap points become brand material, not the whole board.
- Before finalizing any brand direction, be able to answer in plain language: what do you love, how does it connect to your music, what are the consistent pillars/offerings inside your ecosystem, and what does the brand stand for.

## Highest-leverage insight
> Before your next release or rebrand push, sit down and literally write out answers to the two questions this episode gets through cleanly: "what do I love — that I've loved for years, not just what's trending" and "how does that connect to the music I'm actually making." Alba's underlying claim, backed by his own years setting artist fees anywhere from $1,500 to $100,000 for the same hour-long slot, is that the fee gap tracks clarity of brand, not skill gap. Most artists have never written these answers down; doing it in one sitting turns "brand" from an abstract, expensive-sounding problem into a two-question audit you can run on yourself for free this week.
