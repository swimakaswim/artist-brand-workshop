# How to Build a Marketing Team like a Record Label (Big and Small)
**Length / Source:** Episode 51 | Fri, 24 Jul 2026 | 22m | source: buzzsprout
**Thesis (1 sentence):** Content teams should be built in a specific sequence — story/vision first, then roles scaled to the artist's actual level — and the same core process (from solo "come-up" artist to major-label-scale operation) is what separates artists who scale efficiently from those who just hire randomly and burn budget.

## Key claims & mental models
- At every scale, successful teams organize into the same three buckets: a touring content team (fast turnaround, on-the-ground), a brand content team (day-to-day non-tour content), and a "big moments" team (tentpole productions — music videos, trailers, stunts) — framed as an Ocean's 11 crew where specialists each own a role toward one mission.
- Roles don't disappear as budget shrinks, they combine — at the smallest scale, one person (the "predator": producer + director + shooter + photographer + editor in one) fills what a major artist splits across a dozen specialists.
- A creative director's real job isn't "make cool stuff" — it's defining the story/vision for the current era and making sure every subsequent creative decision supports that plan; a brand manager then operationalizes that vision by managing the specialist teams under it.
- Talented people alone aren't enough — if everyone is skilled but moving in different directions without a manager aligning them, the plan breaks.
- The most common failure mode isn't lack of resources, it's lack of clarity: artists who stall usually either don't have a defined story/content strategy, or don't have the right people to execute it — and a confused audience doesn't grow.
- For an artist who IS the brand, the single best investment is finding (or becoming) "the predator" before hiring narrower specialists.
- Growing artists should personally own the project manager and data analyst functions themselves before delegating — define what you do best, then find specialists for the rest, rather than trying to be everywhere doing everything.
- Cost efficiency at scale comes from two levers: better sequencing/structure (approvals, data, communication systems) and consolidating many single-purpose shoots into fewer "super shoots" that capture multiple deliverables at once.
- The data analyst role (increasingly common at touring/major scale) exists solely to track and report — streams, ticket demand, channel engagement, city-by-city fan behavior — separate from strategy or creative execution.

## Concrete tactics (do-this)
- Run every project through the same 8-step sequence: (1) define the result/goal, (2) define the vision/story/era, (3) production planning (what, who, where, budget, timeline), (4) capture/execute, (5) post (edit, color, graphics, sound, captions), (6) build a distribution plan/content calendar, (7) operations (deadlines, approvals, file org, ownership), (8) analysis/post-mortem on what worked.
- Come-up artist (no team yet): prioritize finding one "predator" over hiring multiple specialists; personally run project management and a regular data-review habit; pick one distribution channel/area to focus on and only expand once it's working.
- Touring artist scaling up: a 4-person touring crew (artist, tour manager, VJ/LD, one dedicated content "predator") plus a lean day-to-day brand team (creative direction/brand manager + rotating freelancers); add a data analyst or brand strategist once the budget allows.
- Major-artist scale: separate the three content teams under one brand manager + dedicated project manager; use regional PR (the example artist ran 5 press agents across US/Europe/Asia/Australia); contract outside agencies per video or per project (e.g., per album/EP) for tentpole moments, but still manage them against the brand story.
- To cut cost without cutting output: audit your annual shoot calendar and consolidate small single-purpose shoots into fewer "super shoots" that capture many deliverables in one session.
- Free resource offered: a content calendar template (comment "calendar" on the video to get it).

## Highest-leverage insight
> If you don't have a team yet, stop trying to do everything across every platform and stop hiring specialists prematurely. Find (or become) one "predator" who can produce, direct, shoot, and edit end-to-end, then personally take ownership of project management and a weekly data-review habit yourself — pick a single distribution focus rather than spreading across all channels at once. This is the exact pattern the video traces from major-label-level teams down to solo artists: clarity of story plus a defined process consistently beats more people or more money.
