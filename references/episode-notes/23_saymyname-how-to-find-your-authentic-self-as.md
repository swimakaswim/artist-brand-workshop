# SAYMYNAME: How to Find Your Authentic Self As an Artist
**Length / Source:** Episode 23 | Tue, 28 Jan 2025 | 59m | source: whisper
**Thesis (1 sentence):** SAYMYNAME argues that genuine differentiation — a genre-bending sound, a deliberately chosen visual identity, and a self-defined creative direction — has to be actively built and defended with self-belief before an audience will validate it, because copying an existing sound or look reliably fails while an honest synthesis of your own influences reliably stands out.

## Key claims & mental models
- "It takes a lot of self-belief to get people on board... fans aren't gonna always see your vision in the beginning" — belief has to precede validation, including from your own fans, not follow it.
- Chasing what's already proven (copying a sound, a look, or a peer's approach) consistently backfired for him personally — it produced a ceiling of "I'm not as good as this person" rather than a distinct identity; the fix was identifying what he specifically does well instead of competing on someone else's terms.
- Genre creation as deliberate synthesis, not accident: "hard trap" came from intentionally smashing two existing lanes together (hardstyle and trap) rather than picking one trend and following it — described as "I want to do what they're doing, but I want to do it differently."
- Active anti-trend scanning: before a set or release, he reads what's expected (what a festival lineup or "the vibe of the night" will likely be) and deliberately plays against it, specifically to be the one people remember as different.
- Visual identity (red dreads, a cloak) is framed as a deliberate "uniform" choice for the project, explicitly separated from identity/race — the point is that it's chosen, consistent, and unlike what other DJs in his lane wear, which is what makes it functionally effective as a memory hook.
- Standing out is now a competitive necessity, not a nice-to-have: he frames a scrolling feed as a direct competition against roughly 20 other DJs' similar-looking show clips, so blending in has a real cost.
- A DJ set is reframed as an intentional emotional arc (reading the room's energy and shaping what comes next), not a playlist of the biggest songs — and he argues the reason people follow a specific DJ over time is curiosity about their taste and curation, not loyalty to one fixed sound.
- Reframing your internal language about the work changes your relationship to it: swapping combative language ("going to war" with a stressful project) for playful language ("going to the playground") is presented as having materially reduced his creative stress.
- The industry tends to treat artists as products to be transacted rather than as an ongoing story — presented as a mismatch worth resisting in how you think about your own project.

## Concrete tactics (do-this)
- Run a deliberate "map the norm, then invert it" exercise before a release or a set: identify what's expected/default in your lane right now, and choose your opposite or a mashup nobody's tried, specifically because it's different, not just because it's good.
- Choose one small, consistent visual "uniform" element for your project and repeat it on purpose so it becomes a recognition hook — treat it as a costume decision, not an incidental style choice.
- If a project or creative process is causing dread, try explicitly renaming it in your own head (his example: "going to war" became "going to the playground") and notice whether the emotional relationship to the work shifts.
- Plan a set (or a body of work) as a vibe arc with checkpoints (e.g., "10 minutes in, the crowd is winded — what comes next to reset that") rather than a sequence of standalone bangers.

## Highest-leverage insight
> Before your next release or set, explicitly write down what "everyone in your lane" currently sounds like or looks like right now — the default festival-lineup vibe, the expected genre choices, the expected visual — then deliberately choose the opposite, or mash two unrelated influences together the way SAYMYNAME built "hard trap" out of hardstyle plus trap. This is a repeatable weekly exercise, not just an origin-story anecdote: the differentiation move is to actively map the norm and invert it on purpose, rather than hoping your natural taste will read as distinct on its own.
