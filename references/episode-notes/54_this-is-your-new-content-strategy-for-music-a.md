# This Is Your New Content Strategy for Music Artists (More Time to Make Music)
**Length / Source:** Episode 54 | Mon, 31 Aug 2026 | 17m | source: buzzsprout
**Thesis (1 sentence):** Instead of reactively creating content day-to-day — which causes burnout and inconsistency — artists should follow a structured pre-production process (story, then sandbox, then format, then "meat on the bone" batching) to capture a month or more of content in a single, meticulously planned shoot day.

## Key claims & mental models
- Content shouldn't be made every single day reactively ("what do I post today?") — that pattern causes burnout and inconsistent quality; artists need genuine rest/recharge time that isn't secretly content-making.
- Proof of concept cited: a fashion brand client went from 155 separate shoots in a year to 12 planned shoot days while producing MORE usable content — purely through planning and optimization, not more budget.
- Reframes content as "communication" — still the core mechanism for building an audience of like-minded people — arguing the real problem isn't whether to make content but how it gets produced (reactive, one-shoot-per-asset vs. planned and batched).
- Pre-production planning, not the capture day itself, is where content quality is actually won or lost — illustrated by a DP's line that meticulous planners make him feel safest, and an unprepared shoot is "like being given a fork to eat soup with."
- The "sandbox" concept: before generating specific content ideas, define the point-of-view container all content will live in (human/real, world-building/fictional, comedic, etc.) — this constrains and focuses idea generation.
- "Format" is defined precisely as a repeatable structure/container/style for communicating an idea (song preview, studio performance, carousel, BTS clip, livestream, recurring series) — the topic changes, the format stays constant; recommends 4–6 core formats as a target, though some brands succeed on just 2.
- The "meat on the bone" principle: before finalizing a shoot plan, ask whether a single piece of content, location, or day can be repurposed into multiple deliverables — demonstrated by the video's own topic becoming 8 different pieces of content (YouTube video, IG article, newsletter, blog, Substack, podcast audio, community clips, future livestream topic).
- Booking a single multi-use location (several distinct rooms/setups within one venue) is a specific, low-cost lever for maximizing deliverables per shoot day.
- The method scales from near-zero budget (favors: a friend with a camera, a gimbal, a borrowed family-friend location) to million-dollar productions — same underlying process at every budget level, just different output volume (a month's worth of content vs. three months' worth).

## Concrete tactics (do-this)
- Follow the 10-step process for every content cycle: (1) start with the story — define the artist's persona, current point of view, message, audience, and desired feeling — before any shot list; (2) structure the "sandbox" (the POV container the content lives in); (3) define 4–6 exact repeatable formats that fit the brand; (4) hunt for "meat on the bone" — ways to multiply or repurpose each planned capture; (5) build a creative deck/mood board with explicit dos and don'ts for all collaborators; (6) pre-production — full shot list, locked locations and permits, sourced props, confirmed styling and crew, and a run-of-show built in 15-minute increments; (7) execute the shoot; (8) post-production; (9) map everything onto a content calendar; (10) repeat the cycle.
- Real client shoot-day example: a 9.5-hour day (plus 30-min lunch) produced a deliverables list of 4 social-show mini episodes, 4 short-form brand videos, 4 studio performance videos, 4 making-of videos, 8 talk-to-camera videos, full BTS footage, and a photo shoot — all from one multi-room location (a converted live room, a studio room, a lounge "vibe" room) — with bonus extras (Spotify Canvas loops, live-show visuals, press/tour-flyer photos) captured the same day at no extra cost.
- Sample run-of-show timing given: hour 1 talk-to-camera videos (lowest-energy warm-up) → 30 min reset → 90 min photo shoot → 2 hrs one-shot brand videos → 30 min lunch → 1 hr social-show mini episodes → 30 min reset → 1 hr making-of/creative-process videos → 30 min reset → 1 hr studio performance/live-session videos.
- Low-budget version is explicitly validated: the same 10-step method works with a friend-photographer, a friend-videographer on iPhone plus gimbal, and a borrowed location — "almost all favors," near-zero cash cost, same process just smaller output.
- Free resource offered: a basic pre-production book template (comment "production" to get it).

## Highest-leverage insight
> Before your next free day, write out a full shot list and a 15-minute-increment run-of-show — starting with the lowest-energy setup (like talking-to-camera) and building from there — then for every planned setup explicitly ask "what else can I capture here for free while the camera and location are already set up?" (a photo carousel alongside a video, a BTS clip alongside the main capture, a Spotify Canvas loop alongside a performance). One well-planned day run this way can realistically replace a month of reactive, burnout-inducing daily content-making — the constraint was never time or budget, it was the absence of a pre-production plan.
