# How Social Media Algorithms Actually Work for Music Artists (And How to Beat Them)
**Length / Source:** Episode 47 | Mon, 04 May 2026 | 20m | source: youtube
**Thesis (1 sentence):** The algorithm doesn't reward posting consistency or push your music — it matches audience identity to content identity, so beating it requires narrowing your brand and deliberately engineering content for the specific psychological triggers behind comments, shares, saves, likes, and watch time.

## Key claims & mental models
- The old belief that consistent posting volume alone eventually produces a hit is explicitly rejected — that is "no longer" how the game works.
- Platforms are ad businesses optimizing for time-on-platform; when you post, content is shown to a test sample (mostly non-followers) and the algorithm is trying to match your content's identity to viewers' logged taste/identity, not "pushing your music."
- An inconsistent brand (no consistent format, style, or story) confuses the algorithm's identity-matching function, so content gets shown to the wrong people and dies after the test phase.
- Beating the algorithm through branding means niching down — not narrowing your genre, but narrowing the specific archetype + sonic/aesthetic blend so the algorithm has a clearer identity to match against an audience.
- Frame your presence across platforms as a "network" with multiple "channels" (like a TV network's different shows) — content can differ by platform but should all trace back to one central brand identity and point of view.
- The algorithm tracks specific engagement signals — watch time/rewatches, comments, likes, saves, shares — and each of these has distinct, listable psychological drivers that can be engineered for deliberately rather than left to chance.
- Artists are often too "soft," avoiding real opinions for fear of alienating people — but a clear point of view (not necessarily political) is what sparks comments and discussion, which the algorithm rewards; trying to appeal to everyone reads as brand-less.
- People share content for identifiable reasons (shock/awe, "this is so me/you/us," emotional hit, prompting conversation, status/being first, social currency, agreement) — content can be built around a specific one of these triggers.
- People like content for reasons that often have nothing to do with the song itself — 10 named reasons include visual appeal, self-recognition, relatability, technical impressiveness, vibe/mood, support for the artist, agreement with a stance, aspiration, and pure entertainment value.
- The strongest content stacks multiple engagement levers into one piece (unique visual execution + a conversation-sparking concept + a built-in reason to share) instead of relying on the song or a trending format alone to carry it.

## Concrete tactics (do-this)
- Niche the brand description down to an archetype + specific genre blend (worked examples: "everyman brand blending melodic house with yacht rock," "rebel-hero brand blending metalcore with cinematic score music," "lover-rebel brand blending glossy pop with pop-punk").
- To drive comments: run a "comment X and I'll DM you the free unreleased track/bootleg" mechanic; or post a clear on-screen opinion/stance designed to start a debate (example given: "the house music of today sucks, I miss 2000s house").
- To drive shares: attach a relatable hook to the song's context (example: "I think I just found the song I'm walking down the aisle to," then perform it) so it becomes shareable for a specific real-life moment; or shoot a well-executed mini music video with a clever concept and unique cinematography people want to share to look like an early discoverer; or build a relatable scenario with on-screen text (example: an "aux cord fight with friends" bit) with the song as soundtrack.
- To drive watch time: open with a strong hook; use "curiosity loops" (state something like "I can't believe this happened" early, delay the payoff to the end, re-hook every 5-10 seconds in short form); follow a setup-curiosity-tension-payoff story structure and cut anything that doesn't drive it forward; build in rewatch value via Easter eggs, a twist ending, or fast pacing that rewards repeat viewing; design a seamless loop point.
- Before shooting any concept, explicitly ask: why would someone comment on this, why would someone share it (and which category of sharing), why would someone save it, why would someone like it beyond liking the song — and try to stack more than one "yes" into the same piece.

## Highest-leverage insight
> Before filming anything this week, run the concept through the four-lever checklist explicitly: what makes someone comment, what makes someone share (and which of the named share-categories it hits), what makes someone save it, and what makes someone like it that has nothing to do with the song being good. Most artists only ever ask "is this a good song/clip" and hope engagement follows — this reframes content planning as engineering a specific data point (comment, share, save, watch time) into the concept itself, which is a concrete pre-production step, not just a mindset shift, and it's the actual thing the algorithm is scoring.
