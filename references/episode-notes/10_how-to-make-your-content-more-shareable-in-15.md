# How Top Music Artists Consistently Go Viral: Psychology of Creating Shareable Content
**Length / Source:** Tue, 22 Oct 2024 | 19m | source: youtube
**Thesis (1 sentence):** Content becomes genuinely shareable when it's deliberately built to trigger one of a specific set of psychological reasons people share (either one-to-one or to their whole audience), then filtered through brand authenticity and creative execution.

## Key claims & mental models
- Core metaphor: brands are movies, content pieces are "scenes" from that movie — the operating question for any post is "why would somebody share this scene from my movie?"
- Two distinct categories of sharing with different motivations: person-to-person (direct share with one friend/family member) vs. sharing to one's whole audience (a repost/broadcast) — content should be designed knowing which kind of share it's chasing.
- Person-to-person sharing reasons: "isn't this crazy" (notable/out-of-the-ordinary), "this is entertaining/fascinating/cool," "I feel cool sharing this" (the share itself is a flex/status move), "this is us" (relationship in-joke), "we should try this" (aspirational/fun experience, useful for show announces), "remember this" (nostalgia), "we both love this" (fandom bonding), "this reminds me of you" (relationship deepening).
- Audience-facing sharing reasons: it aligns with a belief the sharer holds (identity signaling), it's entertaining/cool, "this is coming up" (announcing something the sharer cares about), "isn't this crazy" (Purple-Cow-style notability), or it's simply valuable enough that the sharer thinks their followers want it.
- Seth Godin's "Purple Cow" concept: unremarkable is invisible; something genuinely out-of-the-ordinary compels people to stop and share it for you — the goal is engineering "purple cow" moments rather than relying on paid promotion.
- Formula for building a piece of sharable content: [a sharable concept] + [what makes it authentic to my brand] + [how I can execute/present it more creatively/uniquely than how it's been done before]. Straight-up copying a viral concept is "fair game," but running it through your own brand filter and adding real creative execution is where the actual craft and payoff live.
- Sharable social content roughly follows a mini 3-act structure — hook, conflict, climax — the same shape as a scene in the larger brand "movie."
- This shareability thinking isn't limited to single posts — it applies equally to pop-ups, merch drops, releases, and show announces.

## Concrete tactics (do-this)
- Before posting, explicitly identify which specific "reason to share" (from the lists above) you're designing for — don't post generically and hope.
- Apply the 3-part formula literally when building a piece of content: start from a proven sharable concept you've noticed elsewhere, filter it through what's authentically true to your brand, then push the execution to be more creative/unique than the reference.
- Structure sharable content as hook → conflict → climax, even in short social formats.
- Case study to model: Alice Cooper's Wembley Arena stunt (via the documentary "Supermensch," about manager Shep Gordon) — with only 50 tickets sold two weeks out for a 10,000-cap arena, the team staged a fake truck "breakdown" in Piccadilly Circus featuring a huge nude Alice Cooper/boa constrictor billboard image, deliberately causing a traffic jam and news coverage; parental outrage plus kid fascination drove word-of-mouth sharing and sold out the show. Lesson: engineer one genuinely notable moment as your marketing vehicle instead of just buying ads.
- Research what's currently getting shared/going viral for concept inspiration, but always run it through the brand-authenticity + creative-execution filter rather than copying it directly.

## Highest-leverage insight
> Before your next post, don't just ask "is this good content" — pick one specific, named reason from the list (e.g., "isn't this crazy," "we both love this," "this reminds me of you") and deliberately engineer the piece to trigger that exact reaction, then run it through the 3-part formula: proven sharable concept → filtered through what's authentically your brand → executed with a creative twist that's more unique than the reference you pulled from. Most artists post first and hope it resonates; this flips the order so shareability is designed in from the start rather than left to chance.
